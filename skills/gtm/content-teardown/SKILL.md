# Content Teardown

Perform a structured analysis of a blog post, article, or piece of written content — your own or someone else's — that makes the content's decisions legible. What's working, what's not, and why. The deliverable is a teardown document: a structured critique, not a rewrite.

This is an analytical skill. It produces understanding and specific recommendations, not a replacement draft. Use it before publishing, after a post underperforms, when studying what makes a competitor's content effective, or when someone says "this post is good but I can't explain why."

## Process

### 1. Read & react

Read the content fresh. Before analyzing anything, record the gut reactions — these instinctive responses are data that disappears the moment you start thinking critically:

- Did the title make you want to read? What did it promise?
- Where did you first lose interest or skim? Where did you lean in?
- What's the one thing you'll remember in an hour?
- Did the ending land, or did the piece just stop?
- The "bar test": if you described this post's topic at a bar, would the person lean in or check their phone?

Write these down before proceeding. First impressions cannot be reconstructed after deep analysis — they're gone the moment you start reading carefully.

### 2. Tear down by dimension

Analyze the content systematically using the ten dimensions in DIMENSIONS.md. Do not try to cover everything equally — focus on what's most notable. Every piece has 2-3 dimensions where it's strong and 2-3 where it's weak. Find those.

For each dimension, note:
- What specific decisions the writer made (not "good structure" — what structure, why that order, what's the throughline)
- Whether the decision appears intentional or default
- How it compares to the best content in this category or topic

Cross-reference seo-aeo/CONTENT-FRAMEWORK.md for the SEO/AEO dimension. Use VOICE-ANALYSIS.md for deep voice evaluation. Use EVIDENCE-FRAMEWORK.md for argument and evidence assessment.

### 3. Synthesize

Produce the teardown document using the format in TEARDOWN-FORMAT.md. Lead with the verdict — the strongest move and the biggest miss. The reader should understand the content's essential character in the first three sentences.

Score each dimension (strong/adequate/weak) but don't let scores replace analysis. A score is a summary; the analysis is the substance.

### 4. Prescribe

Adapt the output to the user's purpose:

- **Tearing down their own content (pre-publish):** Provide specific line-edit-level recommendations. "Cut this paragraph." "This claim needs evidence — add the specific number or remove the claim." "This transition doesn't earn the next section." The goal is to make the piece publishable at a higher level.
- **Tearing down their own content (post-publish):** Diagnose why it did or didn't perform. Separate content quality issues from distribution issues — a great post with no audience is a different problem than a weak post with a big audience.
- **Tearing down someone else's content:** Extract the transferable lessons. What's this writer doing structurally that works? What would you steal for your own writing? What mistakes are they making that you should avoid?

## Rules

- Read the full content before analyzing. Don't start critiquing paragraph one until you've read the conclusion.
- Record gut reactions before deep analysis. They cannot be reconstructed.
- Be specific. "The writing is good" is not analysis. "The writer uses a specific customer story in paragraph 3 that makes the abstract claim in paragraph 2 concrete — this is the post's strongest move" is.
- Be honest but constructive. "This claim has no evidence" is useful feedback. "This is bad writing" is not. Quote the content when critiquing — specificity is respect.
- Focus on what's most notable, not on comprehensive coverage. A teardown that says "adequate" ten times teaches nothing. Find the peaks and valleys.
- Distinguish between taste and craft. "I wouldn't write it this way" is taste. "This claim is unsupported" is craft. Taste observations are fine to note, but label them as such.
- Don't rewrite unless asked. The teardown is for understanding. Rewrites come after, informed by the teardown.
